## 1. Purpose and authority

This reference summarises the difficulty and solver skills demonstrated in Mr Leahy's 2025 Year 4/5 assessments and marking keys.

Use these examples to calibrate cognitive demand and markable evidence inside the direct architecture in `q7-q8-problem-solving-standard.md`. Do not use them as a menu of unfamiliar response formats and do not use them as the current curriculum authority. Align all new assessment content to the bundled 2026 Western Australian Mathematics Curriculum.

The older assessments did not always use the present eight-question, 20-mark structure. Preserve the current Q7 = 4 marks and Q8 = 4 marks architecture while adapting the strongest demand patterns.

## 2. Q7 target

Q7 is a transfer and reasoning task. It should remain approachable but should interrupt routine application.

Require students to do at least two of the following:

- interpret a new representation or context;
- select relevant information from several details;
- connect two mathematical representations;
- infer a missing value or relationship;
- compare, order or classify using more than one condition;
- reverse a familiar process;
- explain why a result or choice is valid.

A strong Q7 normally has one central mathematical idea and a chain of two to four connected steps.
It should use the concise, direct main-problem style described in the problem-solving standard.

## 3. Q8 target

Q8 is a complex application and justification task. It should require students to plan rather than immediately apply a named procedure.

Require at least three of the following:

- coordinate multiple constraints;
- convert or reconcile different representations;
- infer one or more missing values;
- sustain a multi-step chain where later work depends on earlier results;
- reverse or extend a process;
- combine concepts within the taught topic;
- evaluate competing possibilities;
- check reasonableness;
- justify a final conclusion using mathematical evidence.

The operation or pathway should not be completely signposted. The task must remain fair and solvable from taught knowledge.
Q8 must stand alone and remain independent from Q7. It should retain the concise, direct problem-solving style rather than become an unfamiliar proof protocol.

## 4. Evidence from the 2025 assessments

### Strong transferable patterns

| Source topic | Upper-question demand | Solver skills to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Angles | Select a polygon satisfying several angle constraints; calculate unknown angles across connected polygons without measuring | Constraint checking, elimination, polygon-angle relationships, dependent calculations |
| Area | Decompose an irregular property into rectangles; infer the dimensions of the smallest enclosing square; calculate additional area | Spatial decomposition, missing dimensions, area comparison, optimisation |
| Chance | Compare two predictions for three coin tosses; analyse the chance of an even result on differently structured dice | Sample-space construction, combinations, proportional comparison, classification and explanation |
| Decimals and fractions | Combine shaded hundred-square representations; express the result as a fraction and decimal; combine fractional and decimal masses and determine a percentage | Representation conversion, common units, part-whole reasoning, percentage justification |
| Fractions | Convert wholes, halves, quarters and eighths to one unit and total them; locate mixed fractions on a finely partitioned number line | Equivalence, common denominators, mixed-number interpretation, visual precision |
| Fractions, alternate version | Combine several fractional categories, find the remainder and split it in a 2:1 relationship; scale and simplify a recipe | Common denominators, complement to one whole, ratio reasoning, scaling and simplification |
| Maps and scales | Work backwards through directional movements; estimate an irregular island's area using full and partial grid squares and compare reference areas | Reverse transformations, coordinates, estimation, partial units, comparative reasoning |
| Patterns and algebra | Track a growing daily total with a changing daily loss; solve a chain of dependent symbol equations | Recursive patterning, organised recording, dependent equations, substitution |
| Transformations | Determine and draw all symmetry lines; decide rotational symmetry and explain the order of rotation | Complete visual analysis, accurate construction, invariant reasoning, explanation |
| Volume and capacity | Convert millilitres, decimal litres and fractional litres to a common form and total them; extend a staircase cube pattern by six levels | Unit conversion, multiple representations, spatial pattern recognition, sequence extension and accumulation |

### Separate problem-solving guides

The 2025 archive also contains problem-solving guides that show the desired ceiling even when their companion short tests use routine Q7/Q8 items:

- **Addition and subtraction:** model two simultaneous skip-counting sequences moving in opposite directions, search efficiently for their closest values and compare synchronised turns.
- **Length and perimeter:** infer missing horizontal and vertical lengths in an irregular rectilinear boundary, then calculate and communicate the full perimeter.
- **Multiplication and division:** satisfy simultaneous creature-count and leg-count constraints, then solve a second condition involving a multiplicative relationship using multiples, trial and refinement or equivalent reasoning.

These are strong Q8 patterns because the student must create a strategy, manage dependencies and justify the final result.

### Routine examples that set the lower boundary

Some 2025 items are useful routine applications but are not demanding enough for the current Q7/Q8 target by themselves:

- one large addition or subtraction story;
- one multiplication story with directly supplied groups and group size;
- division with remainder when the required operation is explicit;
- perimeter of a rectangle with both dimensions supplied;
- perimeter of a regular polygon when side length and number of sides are supplied;
- scaling several recipe fractions when the scale factor is explicitly given and no decision or justification is required.

Use these structures for Q5 or Q6, or add genuine inference, comparison, constraints or justification before using them as Q7.

## 5. Four-mark construction

### Q7 four-mark pattern

Prefer:

1. identifies the relevant relationship, condition or information;
2. chooses or constructs a valid representation or method;
3. completes the central reasoning or calculation accurately;
4. compares, explains or justifies the conclusion.

### Q8 four-mark pattern

Prefer:

1. establishes a viable model, plan or first inference;
2. correctly resolves an important intermediate dependency;
3. completes the full solution accurately;
4. evaluates, checks or justifies the final conclusion with correct units and context.

Do not create four marks by arbitrarily splitting one routine calculation into four tiny steps.

## 6. Weak patterns to reject

Reject or revise a proposed Q7/Q8 when:

- the operation is obvious from the wording and only one calculation is needed;
- difficulty comes only from larger numbers;
- several independent routine calculations replace connected reasoning;
- all necessary relationships are explicitly stated;
- the explanation merely repeats the answer;
- a student can earn full marks without showing transfer or reasoning;
- literacy complexity, rather than mathematics, creates the challenge;
- untaught higher-year content creates the apparent difficulty.
- Q7 and Q8 use different unfamiliar response protocols without a curriculum reason;
- an unfamiliar exhaustive-search, person-claim, card-sorting or proof protocol creates the apparent difficulty;
- the prompt prescribes the student's solution pathway.

## 7. Review test

Before approving Q7 or Q8, answer:

1. What decision or inference must the student make before calculating?
2. Which pieces of information or representations must be connected?
3. Why is this not simply Q5 or Q6 with larger numbers?
4. What distinct mathematical evidence earns each mark?
5. Can a correct conclusion be justified or checked?
6. Does the task remain within the current curriculum scope and taught concept?
7. Do Q7 and Q8 use the main-problem and extension-level styles described in the problem-solving standard?
8. Is each response routine familiar, direct and free of prescribed solution steps?
9. Can Q8 be understood and solved without reading or completing Q7?

If Q8 has no convincing answer to the first five questions or either of Questions 7-8, redesign the pair.
