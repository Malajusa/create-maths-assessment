# Mathematical diagram and labelling conventions

Apply these conventions to the student test and its duplicated marking key. Use the same identifiers and placements in both files.

## Whole-shape identifiers

- Use a lowercase letter when a figure is identified as **shape a**, **shape b**, and so on.
- Place the lowercase letter in the visual centre of the shape by default.
- Keep the label horizontal and clearly separated from edges, vertices, grid lines, measurements and construction marks.
- If a centred label would impede solving, obscure information or make the diagram ambiguous, move it to the nearest clear interior position. If no clear interior position exists, place it immediately outside the shape and use a thin leader line.
- Keep whole-shape identifiers visually distinct from vertex, side and angle labels. Do not place a whole-shape label beside an edge where it could be mistaken for a side name or measurement.

## Triangles and polygons

- Label vertices with capital Roman letters: `A`, `B`, `C`, and so on.
- Place each vertex label just outside the figure, close to its vertex and offset so it does not sit on a line, point, grid intersection or angle arc.
- Refer to a triangle as `△ABC`, listing its vertices in order around the perimeter. If correspondence matters, preserve the matching order in every named figure.
- Label a transformed image with primes at corresponding vertices: `A′`, `B′`, `C′`. Use double primes for a second image: `A″`, `B″`, `C″`.
- Preserve vertex correspondence through every transformation. Do not rename vertices merely because their visual order or orientation changes.
- When two figures overlap, reposition labels locally or use a restrained leader line; never change the mathematical correspondence to avoid a collision.

## Sides, lengths and angles

- Place a side name or side length centred beside its segment, offset from the line. Do not place it in the middle of the figure unless the task explicitly requires that placement.
- When using the conventional side names `a`, `b` and `c` in a triangle, place each lowercase side label opposite vertices `A`, `B` and `C` respectively.
- Put angle labels at the relevant vertex beside an angle arc. Use the degree symbol for numerical angle measures.
- Use standard notation where it improves clarity, such as `AB`, `∠ABC`, `AB ∥ CD` and `AB ⟂ CD`.
- Include units with measurements unless the question clearly states one common unit for the entire diagram.

## Coordinates, grids and transformations

- Label coordinate axes `x` and `y`, show the origin and use a consistent scale.
- Place point labels beside—not on—the plotted points.
- Use matching label size and offset for an object and its image.
- Keep reflection lines, rotation centres, vectors and translation arrows visually distinct from shape outlines and labels.
- Use mathematically meaningful line styles and a legend only when the distinction is not otherwise clear.

## Rendering check

After rendering, inspect every diagram at normal page view and zoomed view. Reject or revise any label that:

- overlaps an edge, vertex, point, grid line, angle arc or answer;
- appears to identify the wrong mathematical object;
- breaks correspondence between an object and its image;
- is too small or faint for an A4 printout; or
- occupies working space or reveals information the student is meant to determine.
