# Year 4/5 ordering and comparing fractions exemplar

Use `../assets/year-4-5-ordering-comparing-fractions-quality-exemplar.pptx` as the user-approved content and question-architecture benchmark for this topic. Inspect all three slides before drafting, revising or reviewing a Year 4/5 ordering-and-comparing-fractions assessment.

## Progression to preserve

- Q1 recognises a common equivalent fraction.
- Q2 compares unit fractions.
- Q3 reads two area models and records a comparison symbol.
- Q4 interprets a quantity greater than one as both a mixed numeral and an improper fraction; it does not assess addition of fractions.
- Q5 orders fractions that sit below and above one and leaves the comparison method to the student.
- Q6 locates proper, improper and mixed fractions on a scaled number line.
- Q7 is one concise, self-contained B-level context problem. Students compare four fractions embedded in a familiar situation and choose their own comparison method.
- Q8 is one independent, self-contained A-level comparison problem. Students evaluate three competing answers and prove the correct conclusion using familiar fraction equivalence or another valid comparison representation.

This sequence covers equivalence, unit-fraction magnitude, visual comparison, mixed and improper forms, ordering across one whole, and number-line location before moving to two independent complex problems.

## Upper-question standard

Preserve the directness of the submitted Q7 and Q8:

- state one central outcome;
- leave the solution strategy unprompted;
- provide substantial blank, unlined working space;
- allocate four marks to the visible mathematical comparison, not to four artificial subquestions;
- keep Q7 and Q8 independent in context, information, method and answer;
- use a response routine students have practised.

The submitted Q8 makes a topic-specific exception to the default rejection of person-claim prompts: the user has explicitly approved a short friends-disagreeing prompt for ordering and comparing fractions when students prove the largest fraction using familiar mathematics. Do not generalise this exception to unrelated topics or use the characters' claims as a substitute for mathematical complexity.

## Adapt rather than copy

Vary names, contexts, values, diagram details and surface presentation while preserving the same curriculum concepts and demand progression. Do not reuse the exemplar's exact questions unless the user asks.

Treat the exemplar as an architecture and content benchmark, not as an exception to current production rules. For new assessments:

- display every fraction with a horizontal vinculum;
- number and order pages consistently as Q1-Q6, Q7, then Q8;
- keep all student instructions unambiguous;
- keep working spaces blank and unlined; and
- independently verify all fraction models, scales, answers and mark allocations.
