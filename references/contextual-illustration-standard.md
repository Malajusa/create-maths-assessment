# Contextual illustration standard

Read this reference when a question may use a real-world illustration rather
than only an abstract mathematical diagram.

## Selection test

A contextual illustration is justified only when it makes a necessary
situation materially easier to understand or supplies information students
must interpret. It must not pre-solve the problem.

Before approving it, answer:

1. What becomes unavailable or harder to understand if it is removed?
2. Could an exact mathematical diagram communicate the same information more
   directly?
3. Does it reveal grouping, an operation or the order of steps?
4. Does it introduce irrelevant objects, visual search or cultural assumptions?
5. Is every quantity consistent with the prose and marking key?

If no material benefit remains, omit it.

## Style

- Use original or distributable artwork with recorded provenance and licence.
- Prefer simple flat illustration, clean silhouettes and restrained detail.
- Use plausible proportions. Use exact proportions only when students measure or
  infer from the drawing.
- Keep one coherent illustration style within an assessment.
- Avoid photorealistic rendering, dramatic lighting, decorative backgrounds,
  clip-art mixtures and character expressions unrelated to the task.
- Keep characters inclusive and natural without making identity the focus unless
  it is relevant to the problem.

## Information integrity

- Do not add countable objects unless their count agrees with the problem.
- Do not show a container level, distance, partition or arrangement that
  contradicts the stated value.
- Mark non-scale drawings `not to scale` when students could reasonably infer
  scale from the picture.
- Put required dimensions beside the feature they describe.
- Use a natural table, graph, schedule or map only when interpreting that form is
  part of the mathematics.
- Do not extract prose values into a labelled list or solution-ready table.

## Problem-solving use

The illustration may establish a context such as measuring, building, travel,
sport, food, school or data collection. The student must still decide which
information matters and how to solve the problem. Strategy icons, named methods,
operation symbols and step arrows are prohibited unless interpreting them is
the assessed mathematics.

Q7 and Q8 use separate situations and separate visuals. Never reuse Q7's scene,
data or visual as the basis of Q8.

## Accessibility and release

- Keep all answer-critical distinctions usable in greyscale.
- Do not rely on colour, subtle texture or tiny spatial differences.
- Check legibility at 100% A4 print size and after ordinary photocopying.
- Reject decorative, misleading, inconsistent, unlicensed, blurry or
  strategy-revealing illustration as a release barrier.
